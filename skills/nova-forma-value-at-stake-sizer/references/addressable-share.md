# Estimating the addressable share

The addressable share is the part of today's hours that a redesign could remove or hand to AI and automation. Estimate it from the kind of work, because the kind of work sets how far it can go.

| Kind of work | What it looks like | How much can go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enabling | Moving, copying, re-keying, formatting, chasing, checking against a list | Nearly all of it |
| Execution | Following known rules and patterns: extracting, matching, drafting, routing | A large part, with a person checking |
| Judgment | Weighing trade-offs, relationships and accountability | A small part: people keep the decision |

## How to use it

1. Split the baseline hours by kind of work. A requirements list or a few interviews are enough for a rough split.
2. For the high case, agree a share for each kind with the user, within the guide above. Multiply each kind's hours by its share and add them up. Mark every share as a hypothesis.
3. For the low case, take about half of the high case, because adoption is partial and early estimates shrink.
4. Check the result against the public findings in `evidence.md`, such as the 40% time cut on drafting tasks (E8) and the 14% gain in support resolutions (E7). A case far above them needs a strong reason.

## Signs of enabling work

- The same data is typed into a second system.
- A spreadsheet exists only to compare or combine what other systems hold.
- Someone chases status because nobody can see it.
- A report is compiled by hand from several sources.
